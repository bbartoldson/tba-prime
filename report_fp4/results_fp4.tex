\begin{abstract}
Asynchronous RL pipelines mix rollouts generated by policy snapshots of
different ages $\Delta_i$, but the free EMA-KL surrogate
$c\,(\log T_n - \log T_{n-\Delta})$ with $c = \alpha/(\Delta(1-\alpha))$ has so
far only been validated at a single, homogeneous $\Delta$. We add a
heterogeneous-staleness mode to tba-prime (per-DP-rank checkpoint lags) and a
per-rollout coefficient $c_i = \alpha/(\Delta_i(1-\alpha))$, so that every
rollout estimates the same $\beta\,\KL(T_n\Vert E_n)$ regardless of its age.
Interim findings: (i)~a homogeneous $\Delta{=}10$ reference run through the new
mechanism reproduces the historical no-centering baseline (peak eval $0.829$
vs.\ $0.827$--$0.831$); (ii)~the calibrated heterogeneous arm,
$\Delta_i \in \{1,4,10,32\}$, tracks that reference essentially point-for-point
(eval $0.816$ vs.\ $0.814$ at matched step $520$) despite a $32\times$
staleness spread; (iii)~per-$\Delta$ telemetry shows the surrogate's relative
error is strongly $\Delta$-dependent (median rel.\ error of means $5.9$ at
$\Delta\le2$ vs.\ $0.19$ at $\Delta\ge21$), empirically confirming the
finite-difference variance warning in the theory note --- yet training is
robust to the noisy small-$\Delta$ estimates. Symmetric NVFP4-emulated
(QDQ) training is stable through a 60-step smoke. Dose-sweep and $\beta{=}0$
controls, which decide whether \emph{mis}calibration hurts, are queued.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

All runs train Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on Countdown with TBA
(no-centering, importance sampling on), lr $10^{-6}$, $K{=}16$
rollouts/problem, sequence length 4096, 720 target steps, evaluation every 20
steps, EMA decay $\alpha=0.9$, on 2 nodes (4$\times$H100 inference, 4$\times$H100
training). Full config listings are in Appendix~\ref{app:configs}.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous staleness.} The inference side runs 4 data-parallel
vLLM workers; \texttt{--rl.staleness-offsets} $o_{0..3}$ makes rank $r$ reload,
every step, the checkpoint from $o_r$ steps ago. Each problem's $K$ samples
come from one rank (policy-homogeneous groups); the training batch pools ranks,
so batches mix policy ages. Verified in the rollout parquets: the trainer's
step-10 batch mixes generating checkpoints $\{9,6,0,0\}$, i.e.\ the designed
lags with the offset-32 rank still in its burn-in clamp.

\paragraph{Per-rollout calibration.} Each rollout row records its generating
checkpoint step; the trainer computes $\Delta_i$ per sample and, with
\texttt{--grpo.kl-approx-delta-source per\_rollout}, uses
$c_i = \alpha/(\Delta_i(1-\alpha))$ (clamped $\Delta_i \ge 1$) in the approx-KL
penalty. To first order every rollout then contributes an estimate of the
\emph{same} $\beta\,\KL(T_n\Vert E_n)$; the \texttt{global} mode (one $c$ from
\texttt{max\_async\_level}) is the miscalibrated comparison arm. Both the
importance-sampling anchor and the KL anchor are the sampler's own vLLM
logprobs (\texttt{--use-vllm-logprobs}) in every arm, per the design review:
recomputing anchors from a single lagged snapshot is incoherent when ages are
mixed. The per-token clamp is applied in raw log-ratio units ($\pm 10$
\emph{before} the coefficient) so truncation is $\Delta$-invariant.

\section{Results}

\paragraph{Finding 1: the reference reproduces the historical baseline.}
The homogeneous $\Delta{=}10$ arm peaks at $0.829$ (final $0.828$ at step 680).
The historical no-centering EMA cells B/D from the ema\_KL phase peaked at
$0.827$--$0.831$ under the recompute anchor and the legacy reload rule. The
new mechanism (per-step reloads, vLLM anchor, raw-units clamp) therefore
changes nothing about the known-good baseline --- the control the design
review required before any cross-arm comparison.

\paragraph{Finding 2: calibrated heterogeneity matches homogeneity.}
The decisive arm trains on batches whose rollout ages span $32\times$, yet its
eval curve tracks the reference nearly point-for-point (at matched step 520:
$0.816$ vs.\ $0.814$; peak $0.816$). This is the ``calibrated $\approx$
homogeneous'' half of the calibration claim. The other half --- that a
\emph{fixed} coefficient degrades under the same mixture --- awaits the queued
uniform-dose sweep ($w \in \{0.0045, 0.015, 0.045\}$) and the completed
$\beta{=}0$ control. Notably, the partial $\beta{=}0$ curve is at \emph{parity}
with the calibrated arm through step 220 (e.g.\ $0.766$ vs.\ $0.764$ at step
200): heterogeneous staleness alone shows no early damage, consistent with the
historical pattern that KL-related instabilities on this task surface after
step ${\sim}300$. Whether the KL penalty earns its keep here is therefore
still an open question that the completed controls will answer.

\paragraph{Finding 3: surrogate quality is strongly $\Delta$-dependent ---
and training doesn't care.} The per-$\Delta$ telemetry
(Fig.~\ref{fig:strata}) shows median relative error of means of $5.88$
($\Delta \le 2$), $2.13$ ($\Delta \in [3,6]$), $0.48$ ($\Delta \in [7,20]$),
and $0.19$ ($\Delta \ge 21$). This is exactly the bias--variance picture in
the theory note's limitations section: at small $\Delta$ the finite-difference
slope is dominated by step-to-step SGD noise, and multiplying it by
$c_i \propto 1/\Delta_i$ amplifies it (per-token MAE $0.018$ at $\Delta\le2$
vs.\ $0.003$ at $\Delta\ge21$). Two implications. First, the $1/\Delta$
\emph{mean} conversion is not refuted --- the bias-oriented metrics shrink with
$\Delta$ as the theory predicts --- but small-$\Delta$ rollouts contribute
high-variance KL estimates. Second, despite a quarter of tokens carrying
rel.\ error ${\sim}6$ estimates, the calibrated arm trained indistinguishably
from the reference: the penalty's group-summed, $\beta$-scaled form appears
robust to zero-mean estimator noise. A practical refinement suggested by this
data is a variance-aware clamp on $c_i$ (e.g.\ flooring $\Delta_i$ at
${\sim}4$), which we have not yet tested.

\paragraph{Finding 4: NVFP4-emulated symmetric training is stable (smoke
scale).} With QDQ (quantize--dequantize to the NVFP4 grid: E2M1 values,
block-16 E4M3 scales) applied bit-exactly to both the trainer's linear-layer
forwards (STE) and the sampler's checkpoints, 60-step smokes on one and two
nodes ran without instability; eval reached $0.62$--$0.63$ by step 40,
consistent with BF16 runs at that depth. Saved checkpoints verified on-grid
(99.97\% of elements exactly; the residual $0.03\%$ is a 1-ulp GPU/CPU float8
tie-rounding artifact of the checker, not of the contract, since both QDQ
applications run on GPU). The FP4 collapse/rescue arms (staleness
$\times$ quantization) are queued behind the calibration arms.

\section{Status and next steps}

Queued (cluster saturated ${\sim}40$\,h): uniform-dose sweep
(\texttt{global\_w0045/w015/w045}), completed $\beta{=}0$ heterogeneous and
BF16 controls, FP4 $\beta{=}0$ ladder and rescue arms. Decisive comparisons to
come: calibrated vs.\ \emph{best} uniform dose (the dose-confound-proof test),
and staleness-$\times$-quantization interaction. Deferred by design (recorded
in \texttt{EXPERIMENT\_ROADMAP.md} \S7): offset--rank rotation, common
warm-start branching, $\ge 3$ paired seeds on the two decisive arms.

Operational notes affecting reproduction: launchers now invoke
\texttt{uv run --no-sync} (a node-local \texttt{uv} re-sync once recreated the
shared venv mid-job and dropped \texttt{flash-attn}); and
\texttt{recompute\_logprobs} at \texttt{max\_async\_level}$\,{=}\,32$ retains
33 CPU policy snapshots (${\sim}264$\,GB) and OOM-killed one control --- all
arms now share the vLLM anchor, which also removes an anchor-source confound.
